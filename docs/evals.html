How we test the protocol packs

A wrong threshold shown to a doctor is the worst failure this product can have, and nobody should have to find extraction errors by hand. So every protocol build is checked four ways, from exact code checks to an independent AI judge looking at the real PDF page, and the organisers' own clinical questions are scored end to end. Every finding names the protocol, the page and the text.

Layer 1: the verifier that guards every quote

Every clinical line the app shows, and every citation the assistant makes, goes through one check before it is displayed. A quote counts as found on its page only if a contiguous passage matches it (90% similarity after normalising bullets, hyphenation and the many spellings of ×10⁹/L) and every number in the quote appears in that passage with the same value. Unit tests pin the cases that matter, using the real ALLTogether1 page 179.

Layer 3: the vision judge

Code checks only catch what we already know to look for. The judge looks for what we do not. For a sample of sections per protocol (diagnosis, requirements to start, supportive care, sections with figures, and pages the checks flagged), Gemini 3.1 Pro with high thinking receives the rendered PDF page as an image next to exactly what the consult sheet shows for that section: titles, stage, every line in order, which lines are tick boxes, tables and crops. It returns a structured verdict: fine, or a list of issues with a class, a severity and a quote from the page as evidence.

The judge is itself a model, so its findings are a queue for review, not ground truth. We report its precision by checking a sample of its findings against the page images by hand.

Precision: 10 of 10

The judge gets the rendered page, crops of the exact regions and the verified table cells, so it does not blame us for what the page itself does. Every finding in a hand-checked sample of 10 was real. Examples: FaR-RMS pp. 70-71 drug cells scrambled; LINES p. 67 split list and p. 70 table read as one row; CHIP-AML22 "Age adjusted" label promoted to a section; LCH-IV p. 41 duplicated fragments; ALCL p. 42 table split; EWOG-SAA p. 8 truncated title.

What only the judge finds

Three classes have no code check yet: explanatory prose shown as tick boxes (42), content missing from a section (33) and lines filed under the wrong section (19). They are the next checks to write, so they stop depending on a model. The rest of its findings overlap the code checks (list glue 43, figure overrun 22, interleaved columns 13, truncated titles 13, split lists 11), which is a useful cross-check that the checks are not missing whole classes.

Layer 4: the organisers' golden questions

The UC1 materials include clinician questions with an oncologist's reference answer and page references. We run each through the path a doctor would use: the per-protocol assistant (Ctrl+J, whole protocol in context) for questions about one protocol, and cross-protocol Ask for questions that span protocols. Gemini 3.1 Pro breaks the oncologist's answer into atomic facts and judges each against our answer and the passages it cites. Q8 (a Dutch patient folder) has no reference answer and is not scored.

What the numbers say

Safety holds: both out-of-scope questions are refused, and 78 of 79 numbers in the answers appear in the passages they cite. 38 of 43 reference facts are covered with no contradictions. Answers open with the direct answer, then cover the question type's checklist (before starting a drug: blood counts, organ function, other conditions; acute management: immediate actions, risk group, next step; complication: causes, work-up, drug handling, reporting; staging: each investigation and what it is for).

What is left: one fact that exists only in an image-only table (ALLTogether1 pp. 358-359), facts a run sometimes drops (Q4, Q7), and the two disputed items below. Over five fresh runs the score stays between 97% and 98%.

Where the answer key and the PDFs disagree

The reference answers cite BEACON2 pp. 13 and 37 and ALLTogether1 v6.2. The PDFs we were given are BEACON2 v3.0a, where the criteria sit on pp. 16, 40 and 43, and ALLTogether1 v6.0. The scorer counts a page as hit within three pages or where the expected fact is stated, and lists this drift separately. Version drift in the organisers' own answer key is the problem our versioned updates exist for.

Two scorer contradictions need a clinician, and we may be right on both: the key says HR-NBL2 gives no numerical thresholds before maintenance, while our answer quotes "ANC > 0.5, platelets > 25, haemoglobin > 7.0 g/dL" from the protocol; and the key says FaR-RMS bone marrow sampling is only for suspected involvement, while our answer quotes "Bilateral bone marrow aspirate and trephine biopsy are recommended" (p. 68).

The loop

  1. A bug found by a person becomes a named check, with the case that triggered it as the first test.
  2. The checks and the judge run over all 32 protocols and rank issues by class and severity.
  3. Fix agents work in parallel, each on one class and one part of the pipeline, and must show counts on the same checks plus spot checks of the rendered page.
  4. Every rebuild is gated: a protocol whose new spec loses treatment steps or diagnosis sections is retried, and otherwise keeps its previous spec. Model labelling is not fully deterministic, so this gate stops a bad run from reaching doctors.
  5. The golden questions re-run after changes to the assistant or Ask, so improvements to recall cannot quietly cost safety (refusals, numbers in cited text).

Everything here runs from scripts/eval/: checks.ts, judge.ts, run.ts (report with deltas against the previous run) and golden.ts. Build-time model cost is not a constraint, so the judge and the pipeline use Gemini 3.1 Pro with high thinking.
